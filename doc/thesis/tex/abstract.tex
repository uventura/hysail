This thesis presents HySail as a distributed storage proposal focused on retrievability, integrity, and algebraic auditing. The discussion is structured around the protocol introduction, its mathematical foundations, its operational flow, the blockchain integration layer, and the results observed in the current implementation. The text examines the roles of Online Codes, block representation in a Galois field, polynomial division over $GF(2)$, and the function \texttt{gf2\_poly\_mod} in the construction of local authenticators. It also analyzes the use of Ethereum smart contracts as a coordination and traceability layer for file registration, provider management, and reconstruction requests. The results suggest that the conceptual core of Hy-SAIL is preserved in the repository, although the current coding flow remains closer to an LT-Code-inspired scheme than to a full Online Code implementation.
